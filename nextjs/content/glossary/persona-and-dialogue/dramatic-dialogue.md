# Dramatic dialogue

**An exchange whose speakers, setting, and actions form part of what it means.**

## In plain terms

Read a transcript by asking what each contribution does: persuade, evade, provoke, interrupt, reassure. Then compare an invented Course TF Assistant’s polite explanation with its attempt to manage a disagreement. The exchange can be analyzed as a scene without claiming that a model is a human actor.

## In the readings

Wilde’s *The Critic as Artist*, Part I, begins with music, laughter, and a request to talk; those actions frame the arguments. *R.U.R.*, Act One, interweaves Domin’s factory account with his conduct toward Helena. The arguments happen through characters in situations, not disembodied propositions.
