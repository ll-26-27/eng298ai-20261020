# The void

**Nostalgebraist’s image for the missing ordinary situation behind an assistant’s speech.**

## In plain terms

A Course TF Assistant can say “I” without having the human setting that usually gives that word its background. The phrase “the void” names a critical way of asking what context the assistant’s apparent identity lacks; it is not a technical component.

## In the readings

Nostalgebraist’s essay, §§6–7 (“someone who isn’t real” and “facing the void”), develops this image through the assistant’s unusual position as a speaker. A course comparison is *Phaedrus*, 275d–276a: words can resemble living speech while having a different relation to their origin and interlocutor. This is a reading prompt, not a claim that Plato anticipated this essay.
