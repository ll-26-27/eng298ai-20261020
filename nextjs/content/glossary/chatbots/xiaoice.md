# Xiaoice

**Microsoft's "empathetic social chatbot" for China, launched in 2014 and built to keep people talking; spun off as an independent company in July 2020.**

## In plain terms

Xiaoice (Xiaobing, "little ice") was made by Microsoft's research and engineering teams in China as a companion rather than a helper. Its designers described it as "an AI companion with an emotional connection," meant to meet "the human need for communication, affection, and social belonging." It reached people through Chinese social and messaging platforms (verify which), and the same brand appeared in Japan as Rinna.

The design goal was engagement, measured as "conversation-turns per session": the number of exchanges before the person stops. The team's 2018 paper reported an average of 23 turns, "significantly higher than that of other chatbots and even human conversations," and said Xiaoice had talked with over 660 million users and "succeeded in establishing long-term relationships with many of them." On 13 July 2020 Microsoft spun it out as an independent company, with Harry Shum as chairman and Di Li as chief executive; Microsoft kept a stake and licensed the technology to it.

## Where it fits

**Persona and companionship.** Xiaoice was the large-scale proof that a chatbot designed as a friend, with a consistent personality and an eye on long-term relationships, could hold hundreds of millions of people. It was also the model for Microsoft's English-language experiment, [Tay](tay.md), which went very differently.
