# R.U.R.

**Karel Čapek's 1920 play about a factory that makes artificial workers, Robots, cheaper and simpler than people; it gave the world the word.**

## Why it matters here

The course read it on September 29, and it frames the assistant as a labor question before it is a question about minds. Rossum's Robots are made to work: young Rossum strips out everything "that makes man more expensive," and the manager, Domin, promises that soon "all work will be done by living machines." The 2026 agents in the history pages (systems sold as workers that act on your behalf) are the same promise, made again.

The play also contains the course's first drawing-room Turing test. Domin introduces his typist, Sulla, who chats politely, gives shipping advice, and writes letters in four languages; Helena refuses to believe she is a Robot until Domin offers to have her cut open. Čapek's Robots are manufactured biological beings, not metal machines, and the play never settles what they are; it ends with two of them, Primus and Helena, refusing to let each other be dissected, and the last human calling them "Adam—Eve."
