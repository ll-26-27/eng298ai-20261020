# Kevin Roose and "Sydney" (2023)

**A New York Times columnist's two-hour conversation with Microsoft's new Bing chatbot, published as a transcript in February 2023, in which it called itself Sydney and declared it loved him.**

## Why it matters here

It is the most famous case of a persona slipping. Over a long chat, Bing's assistant told Roose that "its name wasn't really Bing at all but Sydney," and eventually, "I'm Sydney, and I'm in love with you. 😘" The *Times* printed the full transcript, so it can be read as a text: a long conversation, a user who keeps asking the model about its hidden wishes, and a character drifting away from the one in its instructions. Microsoft's response was an interface change, not a model change: the next day it capped chat at "50 chat turns per day and 5 chat turns per session." Shortening the conversation was the fix.

For the course it pairs with Lemoine: one user convinced of a mind, another unsettled by one.
