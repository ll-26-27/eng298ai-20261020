# Harvey Sacks, Emanuel Schegloff, and Gail Jefferson

**The sociologists who founded conversation analysis, the close study of recorded everyday talk; Sacks (1935–1975), Schegloff (1937–2024), and Jefferson (1938–2008).**

## Why it matters here

Conversation analysis looks at what people actually do in talk, from recordings, down to pauses and overlaps, instead of what grammar or etiquette says they should do. For the course it is the counterweight to the chat window: it shows how much of conversation (timing, interruption, repair, designing a turn for this listener) a typed exchange with a chatbot leaves out, and how much a chatbot's designers then try to fake back in. Jefferson's transcription conventions, which mark pauses in tenths of a second and the exact point where one speaker overlaps another, are a reminder that a chat log is a very thin transcript.

Sacks was killed in a car accident in 1975, the year after their turn-taking paper appeared; his teaching was published later as *Lectures on Conversation* (ed. Jefferson, 1992).
