# The Critic as Artist

**Oscar Wilde's dialogue between Gilbert and Ernest arguing that criticism is itself creation, and that a mask lets a speaker tell the truth.**

## Why it matters here

Two of its claims bear directly on chatbots. The first is about masks: "Man is least himself when he talks in his own person. Give him a mask, and he will tell you the truth." Every persona in these experiments is a mask, and the course asks what, if anything, it lets through. The second is about dialogue as a form: Gilbert praises it because through it a writer "can both reveal and conceal himself," and Ernest replies that it also lets him "invent an imaginary antagonist." A chat with an assistant is a dialogue in which one side has been invented.

The essay also matters for historical chatbots. If criticism is "a creation within a creation," then a chatbot that speaks as Wilde is not a recovery of Wilde but someone's reading of him, and should say so.
