# Robot (the word)

**A word coined for Čapek's R.U.R. from the Czech robota, forced labor: the robot was named as a worker before it was imagined as a machine.**

## Why it matters here

The names given to AI systems carry a theory of what they are for. "Robot" says forced worker; "assistant" says helper in a subordinate role; "agent" says someone who acts on your behalf; "companion" says a friend. The course treats each name as a choice with a history. *Robota* in Czech is the labor a serf owed a lord (corvée), and the play's English acting edition glosses it simply: "'Robot' is a Czech word meaning 'worker'."

Karel Čapek said the word was his brother's. In a 1933 newspaper note, "O slově robot," he recalled that he had been thinking of calling the creatures *laboři* (from Latin *labor*) and that Josef, a painter, suggested "roboti."
