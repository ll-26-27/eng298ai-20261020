# Phaedrus

**Plato's dialogue in which Socrates asks what makes speech an art, and argues that written words, unlike a living speaker, cannot answer back or choose their listener.**

## Why it matters here

The *Phaedrus* is the oldest text in the course to ask the question every chatbot raises: what is the difference between words that respond and words that only seem to? Socrates' complaint is that writing "always gives one unvarying answer." A chatbot is the first kind of writing that answers back, so it lands exactly on his distinction, and the course uses it to ask whether answering is the same as understanding.

It also opens with a performance of someone else's text. Phaedrus has spent the morning with the orator Lysias and is carrying the written speech under his cloak, meaning to rehearse it; Socrates makes him read it out. A speech written by one person and voiced by another is close to what a [system prompt](../persona-and-dialogue/system-prompt.md) is.

## In the readings

Jowett's translation, cited by the standard Stephanus numbers (Jowett's text has none; the course copy marks only 257c). The syllabus assigns it "esp. 257c onward" for October 6.

- **257c onward:** the turn from speeches about love to the question of what makes speaking and writing good or shameful ([257c](https://github.com/ll-26-27/eng-298ai-context/blob/24df8b8/_context/course-readings/course-readings-md/plato-phaedrus.md?plain=1#L434)).
- **271d–272a:** "Oratory is the art of enchanting the soul, and therefore he who would be an orator has to learn the differences of human souls"; the good speaker knows which kind of speech persuades which kind of person ([271d](https://github.com/ll-26-27/eng-298ai-context/blob/24df8b8/_context/course-readings/course-readings-md/plato-phaedrus.md?plain=1#L838)). Compare *Analects* 11.22.
- **274c–275b:** the myth of [Theuth and Thamus](theuth-and-thamus.md), on the invention of writing.
- **275d–e:** writing is "like painting"; written words seem to "have intelligence, but if you want to know anything and put a question to one of them, the speaker always gives one unvarying answer," and they cannot tell "to whom they should reply, to whom not" ([275d](https://github.com/ll-26-27/eng-298ai-context/blob/24df8b8/_context/course-readings/course-readings-md/plato-phaedrus.md?plain=1#L906)).
- **276a:** the better word is "an intelligent word graven in the soul of the learner, which can defend itself, and knows when to speak and when to be silent," of which "the written word is properly no more than an image" ([276a](https://github.com/ll-26-27/eng-298ai-context/blob/24df8b8/_context/course-readings/course-readings-md/plato-phaedrus.md?plain=1#L914)).
