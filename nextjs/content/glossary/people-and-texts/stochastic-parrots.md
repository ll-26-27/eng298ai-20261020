# On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots (2021)

**Bender, Gebru, McMillan-Major, and Mitchell's 2021 paper warning about ever-larger language models, and naming them "stochastic parrots": form without meaning.**

## Why it matters here

It is the strongest statement of the skeptical position the course has to weigh. Its central line: "an LM is a system for haphazardly stitching together sequences of linguistic forms it has observed in its vast training data, according to probabilistic information about how they combine, but without any reference to meaning: a stochastic parrot." The section it comes from is titled "Coherence in the Eye of the Beholder," which is Grice and the ELIZA effect in one phrase: the sense is supplied by the listener. The paper's other arguments (environmental and financial cost, the biases of web-scraped training data, the difficulty of documenting it) bear on what the base models in the GPT-2 experiment learned from.

The paper is also part of the history: the conflict over it at Google ended with co-author Timnit Gebru's departure in December 2020 (Google called it a resignation; she has said she was fired). "Shmargaret Shmitchell," on the published byline, is Margaret Mitchell (verify).
