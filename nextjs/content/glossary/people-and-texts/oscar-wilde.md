# Oscar Wilde

**Irish writer (1854–1900) whose novel of a portrait and a face, and whose dialogues on masks and criticism, give the course a vocabulary for surfaces and personas.**

## Why it matters here

Wilde is the course's theorist of the mask. *Dorian Gray* separates a persuasive face from a hidden record; "The Critic as Artist" argues that a mask makes a speaker more truthful, not less, and that dialogue lets a writer "both reveal and conceal himself." Both bear on chatbot personas, which are masks by design. Wilde also wrote his criticism *as* dialogue, so he is one of the course's examples of the dialogue form between Plato and the chat window.

He is also a frequent candidate for a historical chatbot, which raises his own question: is a Wilde bot a recovery of Wilde, or a work of criticism about him?

## In the readings

*The Picture of Dorian Gray* (required, September 22) first appeared in *Lippincott's Monthly Magazine* for July 1890 in thirteen chapters; the course reads the twenty-chapter book of April 1891 (Ward, Lock), which added the Preface. "The Critic as Artist" (recommended) first appeared as "The True Function and Value of Criticism" in *The Nineteenth Century* (July and September 1890) and was collected in *Intentions* (May 1891). See the entries for passages.
