# Alan Turing

**English mathematician (1912–1954) whose 1950 paper replaced "Can machines think?" with a conversation game, and so made typed dialogue the test of machine intelligence.**

## Why it matters here

Turing is why chatbots matter to the story of AI at all. His proposal that we judge a machine by whether it can hold up its end of a typed conversation made conversation the benchmark, and every chatbot from ELIZA on has been read, by its makers or its users, against it. He is also known for founding work on computability (the "Turing machine," 1936) and for codebreaking at Bletchley Park during the Second World War (verify against a biography before citing details).

For a course on dialogue, what stands out is that his test is a party game about passing: in its first version a man tries to convince an interrogator that he is a woman, before a machine takes his place.
