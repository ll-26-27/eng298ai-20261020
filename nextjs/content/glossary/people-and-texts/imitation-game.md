# The imitation game (Turing, 1950)

**Turing's 1950 proposal to replace "Can machines think?" with a test: can a machine, answering typed questions, be mistaken for a person? Later called the Turing test.**

## Why it matters here

The paper opens: "I propose to consider the question, 'Can machines think?'" and then sets the question aside as too vague, putting a game in its place (§1, "The Imitation Game"). An interrogator types questions to two hidden players and must say which is which. Turing predicted that "in about fifty years' time" computers could "play the imitation game so well that an average interrogator will not have more than 70 per cent chance of making the right identification after five minutes of questioning."

The course reads the test as a theory of persona before it is a theory of mind: it measures how a machine is taken, not what it is, which is what ELIZA, Lemoine's LaMDA transcript, and the persona dial all turn on. *R.U.R.*'s Sulla scene, where Helena cannot believe the polite typist is a Robot, staged a version of it thirty years earlier. Turing also answers objections to the idea, including "Lady Lovelace's Objection" that a machine can only do what we tell it, as objection (6) in §6. A competition built on the test, the Loebner Prize, first ran on 8 November 1991 at the Computer Museum in Boston.
